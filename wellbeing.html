Making sense of what you feel

Understanding

There is no single way to experience the teenage years. You can have difficult days without needing to label yourself — and you deserve support before a situation becomes a crisis.

Pressure at school

Deadlines, exams, expectations, and uncertainty about the future can feel like a lot to carry. The World Health Organization notes that schoolwork and attendance can be affected by emotional difficulties. A trusted adult can help you think through one next step.

Friendships, family, and belonging

Relationships and the wish to fit in can shape how a day feels. Conflict, exclusion, or a change in a friendship may be hard to talk about. You do not have to explain everything at once: you can start with “Something has been on my mind.”

Change and uncertainty

Growing up includes changes in routines, responsibilities, identity, and plans. People move through change differently. It can help to notice what has changed recently and who makes you feel listened to.

A small first step

Choose one person you trust and tell them you would like to talk. You can ask a teacher, parent or guardian, relative, or another responsible adult.

When it is time to ask for help

If something is affecting your sleep, learning, relationships, or sense of safety, you can speak with a trusted adult or qualified professional. Asking for help is not a diagnosis, and this guide cannot decide what kind of support is right for you.

If you are unsure where to start at ICHB, visit the school support directory. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 112 in Romania.

Source: World Health Organization, “Mental health of adolescents” (updated 1 September 2025).
